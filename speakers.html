Simon Jäger
University of Bonn
Effective Master Equations for Atomic Ensembles with Cavity-Mediated Interactions and Dissipation
Strong light–matter interactions between atomic degrees of freedom and cavity modes provide a versatile platform for engineering both coherent interactions and dissipation. In this lecture, I will introduce a formalism based on the Schrieffer–Wolff transformation that systematically eliminates the cavity degrees of freedom. The resulting theory yields an effective Lindblad master equation describing the atomic dynamics under a separation of timescales, where the cavity modes evolve much faster than the atoms. I will discuss several examples that can be analyzed within this framework, including the dissipative Dicke model, cavity cooling, and coupled cavity arrays. Finally, I will address the limitations of the method and show regimes in which it fails to accurately capture the stationary state.
JPB
Jean-Philippe Brantut
EPFL
Many-body physics with quantum gases in optical cavities
I will present the physics arising in quantum gases due to strong coupling with light. I will describe the unique opportunities offered by cavity quantum-electrodynamics, in particular the emergence of photon-mediated interactions, for quantum simulation of many-body physics. I will illustrate them with experiments performed in my group over the last years, and then introduce some of the current and future experimental and conceptual frontiers.
PW
Pascal Weckesser
MPI MPQ
Hilbert-space fragmentation and cooperative light-matter interactions in lattice-based quantum simulators
Ultracold atoms in optical lattices offer a versatile, highly tunable platform for studying complex quantum many-body systems. Combined with quantum-gas microscopy, this setup enables site-resolved manipulation and detection on the single-particle level. In this lecture, I will present two recent research directions using a lattice-based quantum simulator with 87Rb atoms. In the first part, we use a tilted lattice to study Hilbert-space fragmentation in two dimensions. Initializing various states with fixed energy and particle number, we observe strong differences in relaxation dynamics. By inserting controlled defects into a non-thermalizing state, we reveal anisotropic, subdimensional dynamics, a key signature of fractonic behavior. In the second part, we study strong dipolar interactions between quantum emitters in ordered, sub-wavelength arrays, demonstrating strongly sub-radiant, multi-photon excitations outside the single-photon light-cone.
